# Clery — Cultivation Notes (Indoor Mini-Greenhouse)

**Origin & type.** _Fragaria × ananassa_ cultivar bred in 1996 in Comacchio, Italy by Mazzoni Group,
from crossing Sweet Charlie × Onebor. Registered and patented since 1998. Clery is a **short-day
(June-bearing)** variety, meaning flower initiation requires exposure to photoperiods below 12 hours
combined with cool temperatures. This is the single most important fact for indoor growers: without
deliberate short-day treatment, the plant will remain vegetative indefinitely.

**Habit & size.** Compact, vigorous, semi-spreading bushes with a slightly spherical shape.
Dark green, glossy leaves. Thick, strong peduncles positioned at leaf level. Produces abundant
runners, which should be removed during the productive cycle to direct energy toward fruit.
Typical indoor height 15–25 cm.

**Fruit.** Uniform conical shape with a rounded tip. Bright red when ripe, darkening to deep cherry
with a glossy sheen at full maturity. Sweet taste without pronounced acidity, pleasant strawberry
aroma. Dense, juicy flesh with no internal voids. Firm skin makes the fruit transportable and
storable for up to 5 days. Largest berries can reach 40–50 g, but typical weight is 20–30 g.

**Temperature.** Optimal day temperature **20–24°C** for photosynthesis and growth. Optimum
night temperature **10–16°C** for fruit quality; lower night temperatures increase sugar content
and improve flavor. The 24-hour average should be around **18°C** for balanced growth and
development. Sustained temperatures above **28°C** stress the plant, reduce fruit size, and may
suppress flowering. Below **10°C** growth slows significantly but the plant tolerates brief exposure
to light frost.

**Flower induction.** Clery requires **2–3 weeks of short days (8–10 hours of light)** combined with
cool temperatures (**10–15°C ideally**) to initiate flower buds. In a home mini-greenhouse where
temperature cannot be lowered easily, extending the short-day treatment to **3–4 weeks** can
partially compensate. After the treatment, return to **12–14 hours** of light. Flower buds become
visible **2–4 weeks** after the end of the induction period.

**Light.** During vegetative growth: **14–16 hours/day** to build a strong leaf canopy. During flower
induction: **8–10 hours/day**. During flowering and fruiting: **12–14 hours/day**. If tracking PPFD,
**200–400 μmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹** supports good growth in containers. Minimum recommended DLI
(daily light integral) is **12 mol/m²/day**; optimum is **17–22 mol/m²/day**. Above **30 mol/m²/day**
plants may show stress.

**Humidity & airflow.** Target **60–75% RH**. Below 60% risk of tipburn and calyx burn, especially
at night. Above 75% significantly increases risk of **Botrytis cinerea** (grey mould), the most
common disease in indoor strawberry cultivation. Provide gentle, consistent airflow to strengthen
peduncles and reduce fungal pressure. Avoid late-day leaf wetting. In still indoor environments,
a small fan running continuously on low speed is sufficient.

**Watering.** Water when the **top 1–2 cm** of substrate are dry. Strawberries have a shallow root
system and respond quickly to both overwatering and underwatering. In cool or low-light
conditions, reduce watering frequency. Overwatering during cool periods is a primary cause of
root rot and poor fruit quality. During fruiting, consistent moisture is important for uniform berry
development, but the substrate should never be saturated.

**Nutrition.** Begin mild feeding after the plant is established (1–2 weeks after transplant).
Indicative EC ranges: **vegetative 0.8–1.0 mS/cm**, **flowering 1.0–1.2 mS/cm**, **fruiting
1.0–1.2 mS/cm**. Target **pH 5.5–6.5**; above 6.5 nutrient uptake (especially iron and manganese)
is limited. Potassium is critical during fruiting and can be nearly double the nitrogen level.
Excessive nitrogen promotes leaf growth at the expense of fruit and increases susceptibility to
disease.

**Containers & substrate.** Use **free-draining** mixes: 70% universal potting soil + 30% perlite,
or similar. Slightly acidic pH is preferred. Pot volume **2–5 liters** per plant, with **20–30 cm**
spacing for good airflow. Do not overpot; excess substrate retains moisture too long.

**Pollination.** Clery is self-fertile (bisexual flowers). In indoor environments without wind or
insects, pollination can be poor. Gentle airflow from a fan usually suffices. Alternatively, lightly
shake flower clusters daily or use a small soft brush to transfer pollen.

**Runner management.** Clery produces abundant runners. During the productive cycle, **remove
all runners** promptly to direct energy toward the crown and fruit production. If propagation is
desired, root a few runners in separate small pots after the main harvest is complete.

**Timeline example (indoor, controlled environment).**

- **Week 0:** Transplant plugs or runners into containers.
- **Weeks 1–4:** Vegetative growth under long days (14–16 h), build canopy, remove runners.
- **Weeks 5–7:** Short-day treatment (8–10 h) for flower induction. Lower temperature if possible.
- **Weeks 8–10:** Return to 12–14 h light. Flower buds become visible.
- **Weeks 10–12:** Flowering. Ensure pollination, maintain moderate humidity.
- **Weeks 12–16:** Fruiting and harvest. Pick every 2–3 days.

**Common issues & quick fixes.**

- **No flowers after months of growth:** Most likely missing short-day treatment. Reduce photoperiod
  to 8–10 hours for 2–3 weeks.
- **Tipburn (brown leaf edges):** Low humidity, especially at night. Provide at least 3 hours of
  higher humidity (>80%) during the night period.
- **Botrytis (grey fuzzy mould on berries or flowers):** Humidity too high. Improve airflow,
  reduce watering, remove affected fruit immediately.
- **Small or tasteless fruit:** Night temperatures too high. Lower night temperature to 10–14°C
  if possible. Also check potassium levels.
- **Leggy growth, weak peduncles:** Insufficient light intensity or photoperiod too long during
  flowering. Increase light intensity or reduce distance to LED.
- **Root rot:** Overwatering, especially in cool conditions. Allow substrate to dry between waterings;
  check drainage.

**Harvest.** Berries ripen from green to white to red. Pick when **uniformly bright red** but still firm.
Handle gently; do not pull by the berry, but pinch the stem above the fruit. Frequent harvest
(every 2–3 days) keeps production continuous within the fruiting window. Clery fruit stores well
for up to 5 days at 2–4°C.

**Safety bounds (policy reminders).** Keep advice within **5–32°C** and **6–18 h light**; present
deviations as **warnings** with brief rationale.
